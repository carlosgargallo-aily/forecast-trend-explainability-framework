Forecast trend explainability

Why the next six months grow or decline

After the monthly forecast is produced, this framework explains the underlying growth over the next six months by linking it to business drivers such as patients and price. It does not change the forecast engine. Sanofi Dupixent is shown as a sample application of the same methodology.

1. Purpose

Forecast charts answer what will happen. Stakeholders also need why the path is rising or falling over the coming half‑year.

This layer attributes that forward growth to a small set of business drivers, shown to the client as relative importance (%), so the story is readable without statistical or monetary jargon.

What it delivers

  • Six‑month underlying growth to explain (context)
  • Importance of each driver as a percentage of total effect
  • Intensity bars from those percentages
  • A simple narrative waterfall

How it fits the product

  • Runs after the monthly forecast exists
  • Independent of which forecasting model was selected
  • Does not retrain or replace the sales forecast
  • Reusable across brands and tenants

2. How it works

1
Start from the forecast

Use the monthly forecast series at the relevant product / market grain.

2
Separate seasonality from trend

Decompose the series so short‑term seasonal patterns are not confused with underlying growth.

3
Measure six‑month underlying growth

Compute how the trend component changes from today to six months ahead.

4
Assemble driver signals

Build features from patients, prices, and their recent growth dynamics at the same point in time.

5
Attribute growth to drivers

A linear attribution model links driver signals to the six‑month growth figure.

6
Present a waterfall

Show each driver’s importance as a percentage of total absolute effect (not volume or €).

3. Isolating underlying growth

Monthly sales move for two different reasons: recurring seasonal patterns, and a slower underlying trajectory. Explainability focuses on the second.

  • The series is split into seasonality, trend, and short residual variation.
  • Only the trend path is used to define the growth being explained.
  • Seasonality remains in the forecast users see in the app; it is simply not the main object of this explanation.

4. What we explain

For each product‑market unit and forecast baseline date, the quantity to explain is six‑month underlying growth:

Growth = Trend(today + 6 months) − Trend(today)

That value is computed in the same commercial units as the forecast (volume or currency) for the attribution step. Clients only see driver importance as percentages, not those units.

5. Drivers and features

Drivers are business signals available at the baseline date. Growth‑style transforms (year‑over‑year, recent change) describe whether those signals are accelerating — which is what stakeholders usually mean by “what is driving the outlook.”

Core drivers

  • Patients — level and recent growth
  • Price — level and recent change

Feature style

  • Levels where useful
  • Year‑over‑year growth
  • Short‑window dynamics (for example last 3 months)

The attribution model uses these driver features — not lagged sales‑trend features — so the explanation does not collapse into “growth is up because recent growth was already up.”

6. How importance is calculated

An attribution model estimates how much each driver feature contributes to the six‑month growth figure:

Growth ≈ baseline + sum of (weight × driver feature)

For each feature, contribution is:

Contribution = weight × feature value

Features belonging to the same business driver are grouped (for example all patient features → Patients). Each driver’s total contribution is converted to a client‑facing importance %:

Importance % = |driver contribution| / sum(|all driver contributions|)
  • Sign is kept separately (supports vs offsets): e.g. Patients +68%, Price −12%.
  • Absolute percentages across drivers sum to 100% (intensity bars).
  • Volume/€ contributions stay internal; they are not shown to the client.
  • Any remainder not captured by drivers can appear as Unexplained.

7. What users see

Users never see raw feature names, horizons, model weights, or contribution amounts in volume/€. They see a small set of business drivers, each with:

Field Meaning
Driver Natural‑language label (Patients, Price, Commercial investment, Competition, Unexplained, …)
Importance Share of total absolute effect, with sign (support or headwind)
Plain‑language note (optional) One short sentence, e.g. “Patient growth over the last year is the main support.”

Default view: a waterfall (or intensity list) of driver importance percentages.

Drill‑down (optional, still non‑technical): expand a driver to see which time lens mattered most — for example under Patients: “Last 3 months” vs “Last 12 months” — without exposing feature engineering names or unit amounts.

8. Sample application

The same methodology applies across brands and markets. As a concrete sample, it can be applied to Sanofi Dupixent at indication level:

  • Start from the monthly Dupixent forecast at sku / country / indication grain
  • Explain six‑month underlying volume growth
  • Attribute that growth to patients and price dynamics
  • Surface a waterfall for each selected product‑market view

Other products follow the same steps; only the grain and available driver inputs change.

9.2 Blending time windows into one user‑facing driver

Measurements of the same driver family are combined before display, so the user sees one label — not four patient rows or three price rows:

Driver importance % = sum(|feature contributions|) / sum(|all contributions|)
sign = support if net contribution > 0, else headwind
Driver shown to the user What was rolled up Importance
Patients Today + last 3 / 6 / 12 months +68%
Price Today + last 3 / 12 months −12%
Commercial investment Last 3 months + last 12 months +10%
Competition Last 6 months entry + last 12 months pressure −7%
Plan alignment Next‑6‑month planned growth +3%
Unexplained Anything left after the drivers above 0%

Absolute importances sum to 100%. In real runs, any unexplained gap appears as its own %.

How to talk about mixed time windows

  • Do not show separate top‑level bars for “patients last 3 months” and “patients last 12 months”.
  • Do show one “Patients” bar (e.g. +68%).
  • Optionally add a short note from the largest piece, e.g. “Mostly supported by 12‑month patient growth, with additional lift from the last 3 months.”
  • Same pattern for Price (“recent price softness”), Investment (“higher spend vs last year”), Competition (“new entrant in the last half‑year”).

Rule of thumb: one label per driver family in the default view; time windows only appear in an optional drill‑down or a short supporting sentence. Never show volume or € contribution amounts to the client.
